# Lanternfolk

A cave people who have outsourced their nervous system to a fungus.

**Why they exist.** Lightless cave systems reward patience and punish everything
else, and the lanternfolk are what fills them: a human frame kept alive by a
luminous colony threaded through the dermis and into the spinal cord. The colony is
the organ. The human tissue is the substrate. Neither survives being separated from
the other.

**What it buys.** Two hundred and fifty years. The colony renews neural tissue faster
than a human body does, so age stops being the binding constraint on what a
lanternfolk can do. They also transact qi with the surrounding air at a rate nothing
else matches, which is why their regeneration is the highest in the species record.

**What it costs.** The colony answers. Introspection — the actual observation of one's
own state without a mediator — is closed to them, because there is no longer an
unmediated interior to observe. They can be extraordinarily good at everything the
colony supports and cannot do the one thing the colony interrupts.

**No ceiling.** Their limit is not a gate. Nothing in their structure refuses a realm,
and this is the only long-lived species in the record that can say so.

**Lifecycle.** Slow and narrow. One hundred and twenty days of gestation, fertility
near a quarter of a Commonborn's, and a pairing that requires finding a
colony-compatible strain inside the first year or the colony rejects the infant. A
lanternfolk child is not born into a family; it is bonded to a strain.

**Culture, if any.** The deep caves do not have a lanternfolk culture. They have
lanternfolk, and then whoever holds the shaft. Light that does not need fuel is the
first thing anybody notices, and it is why every settlement within a day's walk of a
cavern has an arrangement with the lanternfolk rather than a society of its own.
