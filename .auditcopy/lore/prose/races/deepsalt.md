# Deepsalt

The saltfolk of the basin floor, specialised past what the parent body could carry.

**Why they are distinct.** They are a subspecies because a lineage was pushed onto the
saturated floor of a basin whose brine layer is deep enough to hold a body in it
permanently, and then kept there. Every adaptation is the saltfolk plan with the
tolerance turned up and the mobility turned down: a larger sac, a denser crust, slower
turnover. Nothing is invented.

**What the specialisation costs.** Everything that requires leaving. For the parent
saltfolk the brine is a habitat they are badly adapted to be without; for a deepsalt it
is a **substrate**. Taken above the brine line it does not become ill — it stops,
within hours. The parent has nine days. The subspecies has none worth counting.

**What it buys.** The hardest frame in the species record, the fastest cultivation rate
of any saltfolk body, and a brine that carries qi more readily than diluted — so the
subspecies is a better cultivator of the same path and a strictly worse traveller.

**The ceiling.** Ordinal 11, the same wall as the parent: the brine sac runs dorsal to
the spine and the penetrating vessel cannot be rejoined across it. The deeper sac is a
*larger* obstruction, and the honest reading is that they have simply never been tested
at the reservoirs, because nobody has ever needed to take one anywhere.

**What a subspecies is here.** Not a smaller saltfolk. A saltfolk that the basin made
by refusing it a choice. Where a subspecies exists, the interesting question is always
what the parent could not keep, and here the answer is mobility — which is why a
deepsalt is a fixture of a basin and never a traveller.
