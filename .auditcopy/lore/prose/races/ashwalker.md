# Ashwalker

A body that breathes volcanic grit and keeps it.

**Why they exist.** The ash belt is the richest ground in the region and it is
lethal to anything with soft alveolar tissue. The ashwalkers exist because the belt
has to be worked: it carries sulfur rootstock and it is the only surface where a
mineral-processing economy is possible at all. Their sieve is not a tolerance, it is
a business — they turn airborne rock into protein and are the sole reason the belt
has a food chain above the lichen.

**What it costs.** Everything fine. Grit that passes the sieve ends up in the blood,
and the blood carries it to the liver, which is a filter organ, and the liver is the
first thing to fail under the load. The mind path closes outright: introspection
needs undamaged fine capillary beds, and the species spends its life loading grit
into them.

**The ceiling.** The eighth gate completes the ordinary meridian set, and the liver
is the last of the twelve. The ashwalker cannot hold that last meridian, so it never
reaches the reservoirs. Its whole existence is a trade: a body that can eat stone
cannot hold the meridian that lets it stop eating stone.

**Lifecycle.** Thirty-four to thirty-five years of active life. Long-lived by the
ash standards and short by the world's. The bond pair must stay within sight through
the ember season for conception to hold, which is why ashwalker settlements are
sparse and why their households are unusually large — one hearth, not two.

**Culture, if any.** The belt supports grind-rights, not a people. Ashwalkers work
for whoever holds the sluice gates, and the gates change hands far more often than
the workers do.
