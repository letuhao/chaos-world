# Deadlight

Lanternfolk whose fungus died and something mineral took the wound.

**Why they exist.** A lanternfolk colony strain failed in one cavern system and the
body grew around what colonised the wound instead — a silicate bloom able to survive
the same conditions. They are not a designed lineage. They are the lanternfolk plan
with a substitution in it, and they are the most useful record in this domain because
they are the **control case**.

**What the control shows.** Compare them to the parent and you can separate what the
colony did for the body from what the colony did to it:

- Longevity was the colony's doing. Two hundred and fifty years becomes ninety.
- The mind path was the colony's doing. With nothing left to answer introspection, a
  deadlight can cultivate the mind path at all — and does, well.
- Light is not. The bloom emits. The subspecies is named for the one visible change.
- Confinement is the *body plan's* doing. Neither can leave the caves.

So the parent species' single most expensive liability — a permanently closed mind
path — was caused by its symbiosis and not by its anatomy. That is only knowable
because the substitution happened, which is why a subspecies that looks like a
degradation is worth more to this record than most full species.

**What it costs.** Three quarters of a lifespan, a fragile frame, and a much emptier
diet. Ninety years against the parent's two hundred and fifty, in a body that is
otherwise a lanternfolk's. A bloom renews nothing, so nothing in that body is being
repaired — which is the mechanism behind the number and not an editorial remark.

**What they are for.** Not sentiment. They are the only way the deep caverns have
found to keep a lanternfolk-shaped body in them at a population rate that does not
require a two-hundred-year wait, and their fertility is roughly four times the
parent's for exactly that reason.
