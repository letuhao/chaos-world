# Saltfolk

A people built for brine no softer body could drink.

**Why they exist.** A saltfolk is the answer to one question: who can live where
the water is worse than blood? Everything about the body is a consequence of that
answer. Their renal mass is roughly triple a Commonborn's, their skin carries a
hardened mineral crust, and their kidneys concentrate salt to a point that would
kill anything else in a week. That is not a specialisation for its own sake. It is
the only way to live on a pan where the nearest fresh water is nine days away and
the pan never fails.

**What it buys.** The pan is the most reliable place in the dry interior. It does
not flood, it does not drought, and nothing else can eat what grows on it, which
makes the saltfolk the apex consumer of the most stable resource in the region.
Their long service life and complete cultivation paths follow from a life spent
underground half the time in mineral equilibrium.

**The ceiling.** They clear every ordinary meridian, including the liver gate at
ordinal 8. They stall at ordinal 11, the first gate that demands all four
extraordinary vessels closed at once, because the brine sac that makes the body
possible runs dorsal to the sternum for the length of the spine and splits the
penetrating vessel into two halves that cannot be rejoined. The du and ren vessels
they hold individually. The girdling vessel has no continuous path across the sac.

**What it costs.** Total dependence. A saltfolk on fresh ground is dying from the
hour it lands, and no cultivation, technique or item reverses it. Rain is a hazard
rather than a gift. This is why saltfolk settlements sit on the brine and nowhere
else, and why a saltfolk diaspora is a colony rather than a migration.

**Culture, if any.** Saltfolk do not imply one culture. A pan supports at least
three: the well-keepers who read brine and own the schedule, the crystallisers who
work the crust, and the carpenters who maintain the causeways. What they share is a
calendar, not a doctrine.
