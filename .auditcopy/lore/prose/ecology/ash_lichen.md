# Ash-Scoured Lichen

The crust that colonises the volcanic arc's ash fans: grey where the fall has scoured it for
years, black where the fall is fresh.

It is the base of the belt's entire food chain because it is the only thing on the belt that
fixes stone into something a body can eat. Every population above it - the grinders, the
scavengers, the ones who eat the aftermath - is downstream of a lichen.

It is worth recording for a second reason, and the second reason is the reason it survives
betting. The lichen records fall thickness in its own growth. A quiet decade leaves a band, and
the band is legible on the rock decades after the arc stopped being anyone's business.

That is the only long-run record on the belt, and it is kept by an organism that eats stone.

The lichen is also why the belt has a scavenger economy at all. A body that processes stone into
protein is the reason a food chain exists in a place where nothing was killed deliberately, which
means the scavengers here are not competing with predators - there are no predators - they are
competing with the absence of anything to eat, and they are winning, because the lichen is
producing.

The setting's standard observation about this ground is that nothing here is seasonally dormant.
Elemental law governs seasonal cycles and it is unspent on this band, so the lichen is never
dormant, never seeded for a spring, and grows in direct proportion to how hard the ground is
being hit right now. A quiet decade is a thin year. A loud one is the only good one.
