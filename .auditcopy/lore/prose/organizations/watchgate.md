# The Watchgate

## What it is for

To turn an audience into the only currency the mortal plains can levy.

A three-slot mortal frame admits no humanoid, so it cannot produce a single practitioner. It
has nine breakthrough trials anyway, and those trials are the only thing on the plains anybody
would walk a long way to watch. So the ring's value is entirely in the seats.

## What it spends

Iron. Not wages - iron. The ring's standing is built from the same ore as everything else on
the tier, and every place sold is a bar of the seam cut away from a claim somebody else is
holding. The gate is therefore not a business that pays the villages; it is a business that
makes mining decisions on somebody's behalf and settles them in gate fees.

That is why the gate cannot raise its price without somebody objecting, and why the villages
treat the ring as the one reliable income on ground where no tax boundary can be measured.

## What it keeps

A list of places, not a list of names. Seats are resold continuously, so the gate knows who has
paid and not who has watched. The distinction is deliberate: a gate that could enumerate an
audience is a gate that can be asked questions about one.

## What happens to members who leave

They take the buyers' names with them, and nothing else. Places are sold again inside a
season; the gate does not pursue, because a gate that can identify a former broker can be
asked what the former broker knows.

## The two offices that cannot replace each other

The Watchgate decides who is inside the ring. `organizations.reckoning_seat` decides who may be
judged inside it. Neither can do the other's job and both are paid from the same take, which is
why the plains' only real institutions are two bodies arguing over the order of a queue at a
place where nobody lives.
