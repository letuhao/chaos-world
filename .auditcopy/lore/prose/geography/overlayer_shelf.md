# Overlayer Shelf

The Immortal Court's second layer made real ground: ten law slots over six groups leaves four duplicate imprints, and the duplicates have nowhere to sit but above.

It exists because the surface cannot hold them. A second imprint of a law the surface already carries is stronger than the first, and the setting has no other way to say stronger except to print it in a second place.

What makes it worth holding is that it is the only ground in the setting where a law is written twice and the second writing wins - so anything that must not fail is built here, and anything that must fail is built one layer down on purpose.

Ten slots, six groups, four duplicates. The arithmetic is unarguable and the consequence is a landscape: somewhere has to hold the four surplus imprints, and it cannot be the surface, because the surface already carries the original.

This is why the Immortal Court is a two-tier place in the most literal sense. The lower district is ordinary ground that happens to sit under a great deal of law. The shelf above it is ground made out of the excess, and the difference between standing on one and standing on the other is not elevation - it is four laws at double strength.
