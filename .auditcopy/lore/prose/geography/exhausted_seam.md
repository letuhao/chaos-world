# Exhausted Seam

The worked-out face on the Mortal Plains: forty years of open cutting down to stone that pays nothing, with the whole spoil heap still standing on top of the ore that is gone.

It exists because spoil does not disperse on ground with no spatial law to carry it, so the waste is still here in one heap, and a resource can therefore be seen to have been spent out rather than merely suspected to have been.

What makes it worth fighting over is what is under it: the seam is the Iron Grief's ground, and the grief is the exhaustion of the only thing the plains ever sold.

The heap is the point. Elsewhere a mine is a hole; here it is a mound of worthless rock sitting on top of the only ground worth mining, which means the exhaustion is visible from outside and has been for a generation. Nobody on the plains needs to be told the seam is finished; they can see the hill.

It is also the reason the plains cannot simply move on. Advancing the face means removing the heap first, and the heap is four decades of somebody's labour. That work is funded out of the ore it would uncover, which is the trap: the last seam cannot pay for its own opening.
