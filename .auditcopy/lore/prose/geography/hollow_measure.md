# The Hollow Measure

The condition that makes the Mortal Plains impossible to survey: distance there is a matter of argument rather than a measurement, and no two parties who have walked the same ground have ever agreed on the number.

Spatial law is one of the three groups a mortal-tier world leaves unspent, so there is no dial to push and no process that keeps a mile a mile; the failure is absence, not weakness, which is why the same walk can be a day's ride and a week's.

A border nobody can measure is a border somebody keeps taking, so every claim on the plains is settled by walking a marker out and leaving it there - and the presence of that marker, not its distance, is what counts.

It is not fog and it is not distance. Nothing on the plains obstructs a traveller; what fails is the arithmetic underneath the walking. A party that paces the same ground twice returns two figures, and both figures are honest.

This is the reason the plains produce no state. A state needs a unit it can count twice and get the same answer - a field, a length, a boundary in paces. Without one, every office on the plains is filled by whoever is present at the time, and every tax is whatever the collector can physically remove from a cart before it leaves.
