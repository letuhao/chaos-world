# Terrace of Nine Seats

Nine cut terraces on the Immortal Court's high ground, each sized and shaped for one office, and the ground the Nine Empty Seats are observed on.

It exists because the vacancies are real and physical here: the seats are cut, and a seat nobody has ever filled is an engraved absence - the strongest argument a building can make about who ought to be governing.

What makes it worth holding is that a vacancy on this ground is legible from anywhere in the court. The Nine Empty Seats are not a matter of record; they are nine holes, and the whole district is organised around the argument about who steps into them.

A vacant office is a claim. It says a thing exists, that the thing is wanted, and that nobody present has been judged fit for it - which is three separate pieces of information delivered by doing nothing at all.

On the terraces those three pieces are cut into the ground and cannot be revised quietly. A government that loses an office here does not lose a seat; it loses a hole it can be seen to have left, and every party on the lower district is measuring its own position against a shape they did not have to build.
