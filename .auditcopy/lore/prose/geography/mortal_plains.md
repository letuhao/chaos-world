# Mortal Plains

The Mortal Plains are the lowest tier's only worked ground: one sheet of flat land carrying the last unworked iron and the only herb, watched by beasts because a three-slot world licenses nothing else.

They exist because a mortal-tier world spends all three of its law slots on physical, life and qi, which is exactly what iron, a plant and an animal need and nothing more, so the plains are the whole of what three laws can pay for.

What makes them worth holding is that they cannot be counted: spatial law is unspent here, so the only figure of merit on the plains is how much came out of the ground this year, and every claim on the land is settled by digging rather than by survey.

The plains are not a country with a border. They are a claim with a hole in the middle where the measurement used to be, and every dispute on them is settled the same way it was settled the first time: by whoever can get there first and stay.

Nothing here carries an element. That is not poverty, it is arithmetic: the slot that would have paid for an element was spent on the qi that lets an ironhide bear reach a strength worth hunting, so there is no fire that is *fire*, no ore with an affinity, and no way to forge toward one from this ground. An Emberblood body standing on these plains is at full strength and has nothing to act on, which is the reason the Flame Valley works and this does not.

The year is not held by anything. Elemental law governs seasonal cycles and it is unspent, so weather recurs without anything keeping it to a rhythm, and the only clock the plains own is the flood on the Widow Run. A settlement on this ground is revocable once a generation and nobody has ever found a way to refuse it.
