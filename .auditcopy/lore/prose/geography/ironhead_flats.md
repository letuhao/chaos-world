# Ironhead Flats

A broad barren sheet on the Mortal Plains covering the deposit the Exhausted Seam was cut out of, and the last unworked iron in the tier.

It exists because a resource with a visible end is what turns a plain into a country: the flats are the claim, the seam is the spending, and the argument is over which side of them a household may live on.

It is worth holding because the iron under it is the only reason anyone settles ground that cannot be measured, so the population here is entirely downstream of the seam and moves the moment the seam does.

A cart of iron off the flats is worth more than a year of herb off the Bitter Scrub, and this is the entire economics of the mortal tier in one sentence. The scrub feeds people; the flats buy things. There is no third option, which is why every quarrel on the plains eventually becomes a quarrel about where a cart can be loaded.

Nobody farms here. The flats are worked from one side inward, and a family that stakes a claim deeper than the current face spends the winter watching its claim be cut away. The settlement pattern on these fields is a staircase, and every staircase has a bottom step nobody lives on any more.
