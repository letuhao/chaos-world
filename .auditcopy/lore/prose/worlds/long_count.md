# The Long Count

## The other reading of the same budget

Three seats. Six groups. Nothing in the budget says which three, so a world can be opened
wrong on purpose.

The Long Count spent its third seat on `temporal_law` at 0.9. A real clock, exact enough to
date a debt to the day. And that seat was bought out of the ability to be alive, because
`life_law` is now on its vacant list, and an unwritten group has no dial at all.

So the frame is exact and empty. No process runs on the missing group, no substance in it
carries anything, and no life form is licensed by it: no plant, no beast, no herb, no body.
`spatial_law` is vacant too, so the settlement that governed the frame could not say where its
own border was, which meant the exodus could not be counted. `elemental_law` is vacant, so even
a crop could never have been planned.

## Why the ledgers are exact here and nowhere else

`cultures.debt_line` binds a child to a named debt before first breath is drawn cold. It can do
that only in a world whose records survive, and it is the one culture in the setting holding an
exact bookkeeping convention rather than a rough one. It holds that convention because the
households that could afford the stair left first, carrying their record-books off a frame
that could still not feed them, and left it behind sealed.

Everything that is left on the Long Count is machinery. Nobody has been on it in four
generations. It is still running, and it is still exact, and that is the whole of it: a clock
with no one alive to hear it.

## Why it is here at all

`history.discovery_the_law_census` is the first time anyone counted laws against seats and got
an answer that did not change. Once a law's tier list and its seats can be diffed, a third seat
becomes a decision rather than a fate. Somebody took that decision badly, on purpose, and got
a calendar.
