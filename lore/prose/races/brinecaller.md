# Brinecaller

The only body that drinks both.

**Why it exists.** Both parents are trapped by their own element. A saltfolk cannot
leave the pan and dies in days; a tidecaller cannot leave the water and was built for
perception rather than force. Neither can go anywhere. The cross is the single
biological answer to being trapped twice, and it is a good answer — a body that reads
two chemistries and can cross a shoreline without a ritual.

**What it costs, precisely.** Cultivation rate. A brinecaller is noticeably slower than
either parent in the parent's own medium, because it is spending attention on a
tolerance rather than on a specialisation. And the ceiling does **not** improve: it is
still ordinal 11, because the salt parent's split penetrating vessel is inherited
unchanged and the watered parent contributes no help. The cross widened the range and
left the ceiling where it was. Range is not power, and this is the cleanest example of
that in the record.

**Why it spread anyway.** Not for cultivation. For **movement**. A brinecaller needs a
shoreline, and shorelines are contested ground, which is why the cross travelled along
trade routes rather than across them — following the salt-works and the estuaries
rather than spreading evenly. A hybrid's geography is a record of where people had
reason to meet.

**Inheritance.** Freely in both directions, never stably. A brinecaller crossed back
into either parent line gives a plain saltfolk or a plain tidecaller, because the
tolerance is a tolerance and not a body plan. Anyone looking for a third species here
will not find one, and the Tideculled clan's experience is the same lesson arriving by
a different route: a cull narrows a gene pool, it does not build a new body.

**The ceiling, restated for the record.** Ordinal 11, inherited from the salt parent and
unchanged by the water one.
