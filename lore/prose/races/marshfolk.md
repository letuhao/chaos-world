# Marshfolk

A wading amphibian lineage, breathing air, nesting on mats.

**Why they exist.** Every body here solves for a place. The Tidecaller solved for
open water and gave up the body path; the Commonborn solved for the dry interior
and gave up depth. The floodplain margin belongs to neither. It is water half the
year and load-bearing ground the other half, and no existing body works there, so
the marshfolk are what fills the gap: air-breathing, water-tolerant, and built to
cross silt that will not hold a foot.

**What it buys.** Speed and reach on ground that slows anyone else. Qi in a marsh is
drawn fast, because a broad pelvic basin pulls it out of wet ground rather than
letting it settle. They also breed on a schedule nothing else can use, because the
mat carries eggs through the dry season and hatches them on the flood.

**The ceiling.** They clear the twelve ordinary meridians and the four reservoirs.
They stop at ordinal 14, the first gate needing both heel vessels at once. A
marshfolk wades on a three-point stance across splayed toes and a tail-prop, so the
heel never takes a full load and qi never pools there. They are excellent at
walking and structurally unable to stand still, which is a strange thing to record
about a species that spends its life on their feet.

**Culture, if any.** The floodplain has no single occupant. Reed-cutters, fishers,
dry-land farmers on the levee, and marsh folk all use the same water and disagree
about all of it. The marshfolk are the only ones who cannot leave when it dries,
which makes them the cheapest labour in the region and the least powerful.
