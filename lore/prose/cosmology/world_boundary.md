# World Boundary

## Not a wall

A world boundary is the **edge of a law budget**. It is not a frontier with a gate, not a
sea, not a mountain pass. Crossing it means arriving somewhere a law group you relied on is
not written.

That distinction decides everything downstream: the difficulty of travel in this setting is
not logistical. It is a change of reading.

## Why travel is rare

A body is built to the imprint set it grew in. A realm band is a band of *one world's* law
values — so a practitioner's tolerance is valid in the next world only where the two
agree.

Two consequences, and neither is about strength:

- **A void is worse than a weakness.** Arriving where a required group is unwritten means
  the technique that depended on it produces nothing, not less. Full-strength failure.
- **A scar is worse than a debuff.** A layer that once held an imprint and no longer does
  keeps the outline. A body returning to its own tier meets the difference as an injury.

## What a traveller actually carries

Not power. **Tolerance.**

The realm band a body holds is a range of readings it can survive. That range is valid
across a boundary only over the overlap. A cultivator is therefore not stronger for being
high-realm in a strange world; they are *more finely tuned* to one, and therefore more
brittle away from it.

This is the reversal that makes a high realm a liability in travel rather than a
passport.

## Why not a barrier with a door

Because the two sides are not neighbouring rooms. There is no corridor, no distance and no
route. Two worlds are adjacent only where an imprint has failed and both can see through
the hole — which makes **rift** the sole mechanism, and the boundary something that is
*rarely* present rather than *usually* guarded.

## What makes a path open

**One group, one value, both sides.**

Where two worlds imprint the same group at the same value, a body can cross. Where they
agree on nothing, a tear opens onto a wall.

This is the whole reason travel in this setting is rare and lucky rather than rare and
expensive. It is not that the strong cannot cross. It is that the two worlds have to
*coincide*, and coincidence is not purchasable.

## The default state is closed

A world holding itself together is at equilibrium. `WorldState` treats stability `>= 0.3`
as stable, and `trigger_conflict` spends stability at `severity * 0.1` per event — so the
things that cost a world its equilibrium are internal and slow, and a closed world is not
a prison but a resting state.

Which means: **isolation is the normal condition of a world, and a rift is the exception
that has to be explained.** Any account in this setting that treats a passage as ordinary
has an unstated law value behind it.
