# Rift

## What a rift is

An **imprint that has failed.** A law value pushed outside its authored range — qi past
100, temporal past 100, spatial past 10 — so the group no longer holds, and the layer is
left with an outline where an imprint used to be.

It is the only event in the setting that makes one world reachable from another.

## Why that is the only mechanism

A world boundary is the edge of a law budget, not a wall between neighbours. Two worlds
are not adjacent and cannot be walked to. They become adjacent only where an imprint has
failed and both sides can see through the hole.

This is why a rift needs a cause in the authored ranges rather than a cause in a plot.
The ranges already supply one: `value_max` is a real number on every `WorldLawDef`, and a
value that reaches it has no more room.

## What makes a path open

**An overlap.** One group, imprinted at the same value, on both sides.

- Where two worlds agree on a group at a value, a body can cross.
- Where they agree on nothing, a tear opens onto a wall.

That is the whole mechanism. Power opens nothing. A realm-30 cultivator arriving at a
tear between two mortal worlds meets an absence, and an absence does not care how much qi
is behind it.

## What closes it

The value coming back inside the range.

So rifts close on the same cycle tides run on — which is why no rift in this setting has
stayed open longer than a few tides, and why a "permanent passage" is always a claim
about a law value rather than a permanent hole.

## Why the big worlds tear first

A mortal-tier world holds three imprints across one layer. There is nothing left to
overpress.

A transcendent-tier world holds fifteen imprints across three layers, with `temporal_law`
readable to 100 and `spatial_law` to 10. That is fifteen chances to push one dial off its
end, and enough imprints gathered close enough for the failure to matter.

**Rifts are a cost of having a large budget.** The worlds that can reach each other are
the ones that can afford to stop holding themselves together.

## What crossing costs

The traveller's own imprint set is the thing at risk.

A body is built to the imprint list it grew in. Arriving where a group it relied on is
unwritten is not a debuff with a duration — it is a law void, and in a void a technique
fails at full strength. Coming back the other way meets the **law scar** left where the
departure dropped something.

## The asymmetry

A world holding itself together is at equilibrium and has no reason to tear. So rifts are
events, not features: they begin, they run on a cycle, they close. Every culture that
treats one as permanent is making a claim about a law value, and that claim is the thing
worth arguing about.
