# Reading the Sixth Seat

The second law census, and the one that produced a method instead of a number.

- **The method.** Seat a law. Write down what became possible that was not possible before. Then
  write down what became possible before that was seated. Both lists are true and they point at
  each other. The disagreement is kept, not resolved.
- **The negative finding.** Seats above six do not open new paths. They permit one thing: holding
  a law apart from its effect long enough to look at it.
- **Why it is decisive.** To study a law you must not be saturating in it. At three seats you are
  inside the frame and cannot see it. At six you can see all six and stand in all six. Only above
  six can you separate one from the others and inspect it in isolation.
- **What follows from it.** Observation, not cultivation, is the scarce activity in this setting.
  It is why the top of the ladder is occupied by the path that is built for looking.

The finding also explains why the setting has schools rather than just masters: a thing you had to
observe in isolation is a thing you had to teach.
