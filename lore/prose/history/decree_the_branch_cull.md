# The Branch Cull

- **The pressure.** Cultivation costs money per person, per attempt, and the attempts fail. A
  house with five branches is paying five times for one success.
- **The instrument.** Cull the branches. Keep the one that can afford the next gate.
- **The mechanism that makes it work.** A line held pure inside a single household concentrates; the
  same line spread across a village averages with everything the village took in. Culling does not
  improve a gene pool. It narrows one, and a narrowed pool crosses a bar sooner.
- **The residue.** The narrowed line's grant is sustained effort over sudden effort. That is what
  you get when the pressure was continuous debt rather than a single decisive moment.

Every cull was a household deciding that its own relatives were a maintenance cost. That is why it
is recorded as a decree and not as a custom: it needed someone to sign.
